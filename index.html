Germanium chunks into 1 kg bars with a moving induction coil

The graphite boat of chunks stays still inside an argon-filled quartz tube. A short water-cooled coil rides on a motor-driven carriage and carries a narrow molten zone along the boat: chunks melt into its front and a solid bar freezes behind it.

Process, step by step

One run takes about 9.2 hours from purge to opening, most of it the two passes. Steps with an orange number happen with RF on or the tube hot. The animation at the top of the page plays the same ten steps, and the marker on this chart follows it.

What you need

  • Zone-melting unit: quartz tube, carriage with coil and heat station, linear drive and guard, as on the drawings
  • Induction generator: 10 kW, 150–400 kHz, with a remote heat station
  • Chiller: ≥ 7 kW, ≥ 15 L/min at 3 bar
  • Graphite boats: 2, high purity, baked
  • Argon 5.0: cylinder, regulator, gas panel with mass-flow controller, oil bubbler vented outside
  • Pyrometer: two-colour, 700–1500 °C, on the carriage
  • Scale: 0.1 g resolution for the loads and the bars
  • Protection: face shield, heat-resistant gloves, clean nitrile gloves

    Step 1: Sort and weigh

    Keep one grade and one source per melt. Leave out coated lenses and windows, and any piece with solder, glue or metal attached.

    Load
    1.000 kg
    Record
    weight and source

    Single-crystal pieces you could sell as crystals stay out: melting turns them into ordinary polycrystalline germanium.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Clean and dry

    Wash off dirt and grease, rinse in isopropanol, then dry at 100–120 °C for an hour. From here on, handle the chunks, boat and tube with clean nitrile gloves.

    Dry
    1 h at 100–120 °C

    No water left anywhere: moisture near molten germanium spits, and it oxidises the melt.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Prepare the boat

    Use a boat that was baked empty for 2 hours at 1050 °C under argon or vacuum and kept sealed in a clean bag. Keep a second baked boat ready, so a run never waits for one.

    Bake
    2 h at 1050 °C
    Boats
    2: one in use, one ready
  4. 4

    Step 4: Load the boat

    Put the bigger chunks in first and the small ones on top, all below the rim. One kilogram of loose chunks takes about 340 cm³ and the cavity holds 365 cm³. Take off the inlet cap, set the boat in the tube and push it in with the quartz push rod until the stop collar touches the flange. Refit the cap.

    Boat at
    X 265–545 mm
    Coil
    parked at X 200

    Nothing made of copper, brass or other metal goes inside the tube.

  5. 5

    Step 5: Seal and purge

    With everything cold and the coil parked, run argon at 2.0 L/min for 30 minutes. That replaces the air in the tube about 17 times.

    Argon
    2.0 L/min
    Time
    30 min

    The bubbler bubbles steadily, and soapy water on the joints shows no leaks.

  6. 6

    Step 6: Start the zone

    Turn the argon down to 0.2 L/min and check that cooling water flows through the generator and the coil: RF can only come on when both flow switches are made. The carriage moves the coil to X 285. Ramp the power to about 5 kW over 10 minutes and hold for 15, until a molten zone 30–40 mm long spans the boat.

    Argon
    0.2 L/min
    Power
    ≈ 5 kW
    Takes
    25 min

    As the first chunks melt, the pyrometer should read 938 ± 15 °C, the melting point of germanium.

  7. 7

    Step 7: Pass 1: chunks into a bar

    The coil travels from X 285 to X 565 at 1.0 mm/min. Chunks ahead of the zone melt into its front, and behind it the bar freezes from the inlet end onwards. Germanium grows about 5 % as it freezes; the open top of the boat gives it room.

    Speed
    1.0 mm/min
    Travel
    280 mm
    Takes
    ≈ 4.7 h

    Keep the zone 30–40 mm long: if it grows longer, lower the power a little; if the chunks ahead stop melting, raise it.

  8. 8

    Step 8: Pass 2: level and clean

    At X 565 the RF goes off and the end of the bar freezes. Only then does the carriage return to X 285, at 300 mm/min. Restart the zone (ramp 5 minutes, hold 5), then make the second pass at 2.0 mm/min. It levels the bar, and impurities that prefer the liquid ride along to the far end.

    Speed
    2.0 mm/min
    Return
    300 mm/min, RF off
    Takes
    ≈ 2.6 h

    For a purer bar, add more passes at 2–3 mm/min, always in the same direction.

  9. 9

    Step 9: Cool

    RF off and the coil back to park. Keep argon at 0.2 L/min until the tube is below 200 °C.

    Argon
    0.2 L/min
    Until
    below 200 °C
    Takes
    ≈ 1 h

    Never stop the argon while the tube is hot: oil is sucked back from the bubbler and air gets in.

  10. 10

    Step 10: Unload, crop, weigh, test

    Take off the inlet cap and pull the boat out with the hook through the Ø6 hole in its end wall, then lift the bar out. If purity matters, cut off the last-frozen 20–30 mm, where impurities collect, and keep it for a later scrap melt.

    Bar
    ≈ 260 × 40/30 × 21 mm
    Cycle
    ≈ 9.2 h

    Weigh everything against step 1. Before selling the bar as a grade, have a sample analysed (GDMS) and measure its resistivity: intrinsic germanium reads about 47 Ω·cm at room temperature.

Induction coil and generator

Dos and don'ts

✓Do

  • Start the chiller and check both flow switches before RF goes on.
  • Purge at 2 L/min for 30 minutes with the tube cold, and keep argon flowing until it is below 200 °C.
  • Keep one grade per melt and record the weight going in and coming out.
  • Wash, rinse and fully dry the chunks, the boat and every tool.
  • Use a baked boat, and keep a second one baked and ready.
  • Run every pass in the same direction, inlet to outlet.
  • Set the power so the zone stays 30–40 mm long, and check the pyrometer reads 938 ± 15 °C as the first chunks melt.
  • Check the 10 mm gap between coil and tube along the whole travel before the first run.
  • Keep the guard closed while RF is on, and measure the field at the operator position once at full power.
  • Bond the frame, carriage and generator to earth, and test every interlock at commissioning.
  • Have the bar analysed and its resistivity measured before selling it as a grade.

✗Don't

  • Switch on RF without cooling water: the coil overheats within seconds.
  • Move the carriage backwards with RF on: the zone would carry impurities back into the bar.
  • Let the coil touch the quartz tube, or run with less than 9 mm radial gap.
  • Bring metal within 150 mm of the coil: rings, watches, tools and screws all heat up.
  • Put copper, brass or other metals inside the tube or the gas lines.
  • Mix grades, coated optics, or pieces with solder or glue into one melt.
  • Melt single-crystal pieces you could sell as crystals.
  • Run more than 0.2 L/min of argon while the tube is hot, or block the exhaust.
  • Switch off the argon while the tube is hot.
  • Open the tube above 200 °C, or cool a bar in water.
  • Touch the quartz tube with bare hands: skin salts make it go cloudy and weak when hot.
  • Work near the coil with an active medical implant such as a pacemaker.

Safety and assumptions

Radio-frequency power

The generator puts several kilowatts at 150–400 kHz into the coil. Never touch the coil, its leads or the heat station while RF can be on, and keep the guard closed. Measure the field at the operator position, mark the keep-out distance, and keep people with active medical implants away.

Water next to power

Cooling water runs right beside high-frequency power. Check hoses and fittings before every run; the leak sensor in the drip tray and the flow switches stop the RF. Bond the frame, carriage and generator to earth.

Gas and heat

Argon can push oxygen out of a small room: ventilate it, fit the oxygen monitor (alarm at 19.5 %), and vent the bubbler outside. Wear a face shield and heat-resistant gloves at the tube, and keep water away from molten metal.

What these numbers assume

A 380–415 V three-phase supply. Generator power, frequency, zone length and temperatures are design estimates: the generator maker confirms the coil and the tuning with a boat loaded, and the power set point is fixed at commissioning. Items marked TBC on the drawings are for the supplier to confirm.
