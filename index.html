Remelting germanium chunks into 1 kg ingots

Clean chunks go into a graphite boat inside an argon-filled quartz tube. The furnace melts them at 980–1000 °C, then the boat is pulled slowly out of the hot zone so the bar freezes from one end.

Process, step by step

One run takes about 18 hours from purge to opening, most of it the slow freeze. Steps with an orange number happen while the furnace is hot.

What you need

  • Tube furnace: single zone, 2 kW, as specified below
  • Quartz tube: Ø80 × 4 × 1300 mm, with two sealed end caps
  • Graphite boat: 280 × 60 × 44 mm, high purity
  • Pull rod and drive: Ø8 quartz rod, slow stepper and lead screw
  • Argon: 99.999 %, regulator and a 0–2.5 L/min flowmeter
  • Oil bubbler: on the exhaust, vented outside
  • Scale: 0.1 g resolution for the loads and the bar
  • Protection: face shield, heat-resistant gloves, clean nitrile gloves

    Step 1: Sort and weigh

    Keep one grade and one source per melt. Leave out coated lenses and windows, and any piece with solder, glue or metal attached.

    1.000 kg

    Single-crystal pieces you could sell as crystals stay out. Melting makes them ordinary polycrystalline germanium.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Clean and dry

    Wash off dirt and grease, rinse in isopropanol, then dry at 100–120 °C for an hour. From here on, handle the chunks, boat and tube with clean nitrile gloves.

    Dry
    1 h at 100–120 °C

    No water left anywhere: moisture near molten metal spits, and it oxidises the germanium.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Prepare the boat

    A new boat gets baked empty for 2 hours at 1050 °C under argon, then cooled under argon. Hook the pull rod through the 6 mm hole in its outlet-end wall.

    Bake
    2 h at 1050 °C, new boats only
    Pull rod
    Ø8 quartz or alumina, ≥ 0.8 m
  4. 4

    Step 4: Load the boat

    Put the bigger chunks in first and the small ones on top, all below the rim. One kilogram of loose chunks takes about 340 cm³, and the cavity holds 365 cm³. Slide the boat in until it is centred in the hot zone.

    Boat centre
    525 mm from the inlet end of the tube
  5. 5

    Step 5: Seal and purge

    Close both end caps and run argon at 2 L/min for 30 minutes while everything is still cold. That replaces the air about 11 times.

    Argon
    2 L/min
    Time
    30 min

    The bubbler bubbles steadily, and soapy water on the joints shows no leaks.

  6. 6

    Step 6: Heat

    Turn the argon down to 0.2 L/min, then ramp the furnace at 5 °C per minute to 980–1000 °C. The slow ramp protects the alumina core tube.

    Argon
    0.2 L/min
    Ramp
    5 °C/min
    Takes
    ≈ 3.2 h
  7. 7

    Step 7: Melt and hold

    Hold at the set point until every chunk has melted, then for another 20–30 minutes.

    Set point
    980–1000 °C
    Hold
    ≈ 45 min in all
  8. 8

    Step 8: Freeze from one end

    Pull the boat towards the outlet with a slow motor drive. The end that leaves the hot zone first freezes first, and the freezing front moves back along the bar. Germanium grows about 5 % as it freezes, and this way the liquid always has room above it.

    Speed
    ≈ 4 cm/h
    Travel
    370 mm
    Takes
    ≈ 9 h
  9. 9

    Step 9: Cool

    Once the whole boat is past the 938 °C point, switch the heater off and let it cool with the argon still at 0.2 L/min.

    Until
    below 200 °C
    Takes
    ≈ 4 h

    Never stop the argon while the tube is hot: oil gets sucked back from the bubbler and air gets in.

  10. 10

    Step 10: Unload, crop, weigh, test

    Below 200 °C, open the outlet cap, slide the boat out and lift the bar. If purity matters, cut 20–30 mm off the end that froze last, where most impurities collect, and keep it for a later scrap melt.

    Bar
    ≈ 260 × 40/30 × 21 mm

    Weigh everything against step 1. Before selling the bar as a grade, have a sample analysed (GDMS) and measure its resistivity: pure germanium reads about 50 Ω·cm at room temperature.

Wiring

Building the heater

  1. Wind the wire under light tension on the core, holding the pitch with a spiral-grooved tube or a spacer cord. Lock each end with two close turns.
  2. Bring each end out as a tail folded three times and twisted, so it runs cooler, to a ceramic terminal block on the shell.
  3. Cover the winding with 3–5 mm of alumina cement. Air-dry it for 24 hours, then 2 hours at 100 °C and 2 hours at 300 °C.
  4. Wrap 25 mm of alumina-fibre blanket, then 100 mm of ceramic-fibre blanket, and close both ends with 75 mm fibre board.
  5. Pre-oxidise the new winding in air for 7–10 hours at 1050 °C, with the quartz tube out. This builds the oxide layer that protects the wire.
  6. Check that the cold resistance is about 25.4 Ω, the winding-to-shell insulation is above 1 MΩ, and the cutout trips.

Argon

Argon keeps air off the hot germanium and graphite. Flow fast while the tube is cold, then slow while it is hot.

Dos and don'ts

✓Do

  • Keep one grade per melt and record the weight going in and coming out.
  • Wash, rinse and fully dry the chunks, the boat and every tool.
  • Bake a new boat at 1050 °C under argon before its first melt.
  • Purge at 2 L/min for 30 minutes before heating, and keep argon flowing until the tube is below 200 °C.
  • Ramp at 5 °C per minute or slower to protect the alumina core tube.
  • Freeze from one end with a slow motor-driven pull.
  • Map your furnace's temperature along the tube with a thermocouple before the first melt.
  • Pre-oxidise a new heater winding for 7–10 hours at 1050 °C in air.
  • Watch that the bubbler keeps bubbling during the whole run.
  • Earth the shell, fit an RCD and test the over-temperature cutout before every long run.
  • Have the new bar analysed and its resistivity measured before selling it as a grade.

✗Don't

  • Melt in open air or with a torch: germanium oxidises and fumes, and the graphite burns away.
  • Mix grades, coated optics, or pieces with solder or glue into one melt.
  • Melt single-crystal pieces you could sell as crystals.
  • Use metal pots or clay foundry crucibles: germanium alloys with metals and picks up impurities.
  • Let the whole boat cool at once: a crust forms and the trapped liquid cracks the bar.
  • Run more than 0.2 L/min of argon while the tube is hot.
  • Block the exhaust or let the tube build up more than 0.2 bar.
  • Switch off the argon while the tube is hot.
  • Open the tube above 200 °C, or cool a bar in water.
  • Touch the quartz tube with bare hands: skin salts make it go cloudy and weak when hot.
  • Power the winding without a breaker, RCD, earth and independent cutout.

Safety and assumptions

Before the first power-on

The heater winding carries full mains voltage. Earth the steel shell and frame, fit the RCD, and have an electrician check the wiring. Do not run the long freeze unattended unless the independent over-temperature cutout is fitted and tested.

Gas and heat

Argon can push oxygen out of a small room, so ventilate it and vent the bubbler outside. Wear a face shield and heat-resistant gloves near the hot furnace, and keep water away from molten metal.

What these numbers assume

The heater is designed for 230 V mains. The temperature profile along the tube is an estimate: the freeze plan assumes the tube passes 938 °C about 200 mm past the furnace centre, so measure your own furnace with a thermocouple and adjust the pull distance. All figures are design starting points from standard material data; confirm them with your suppliers.
